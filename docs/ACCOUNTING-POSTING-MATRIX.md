# Accounting Posting Matrix

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the accounting events and expected debit and credit treatments generated by Smart ERP business transactions.

The objective is to establish consistent accounting rules across all modules while allowing each company to configure its own Chart of Accounts, accounting policies, and applicable tax treatments.

## 2. Core Accounting Rules

- All accounting entries must be generated through the Central Accounting Posting Engine.
- Operational modules must not independently bypass the posting engine.
- Account determination must use configurable account mappings.
- No account codes or account IDs should be hard-coded in application logic.
- Each accounting event must have a unique source reference.
- All journal entries must balance in the company's functional currency.
- Tax treatment must be configurable by country, tax type, transaction type, and effective date.
- Accounting rules must support effective dates and version history.
- Posted journal entries must not be edited or deleted.
- Corrections must be processed through approved reversal or adjustment entries.
- Each accounting event must maintain a traceable link to its originating business document.

## 3. Account Mapping Configuration

The system must support configurable mappings for:

- Accounts Receivable.
- Accounts Payable.
- Sales Revenue.
- Sales Returns.
- Sales Discounts.
- Cost of Goods Sold.
- Inventory.
- Inventory in Transit.
- Goods Received Not Invoiced (GRNI).
- Purchase Price Variance.
- Input Tax and Output Tax.
- Cash and Bank Accounts.
- Fixed Assets.
- Accumulated Depreciation.
- Depreciation Expense.
- Work in Progress (WIP).
- Production Variances.
- Payroll Expenses and Liabilities.
- Foreign Exchange Gains and Losses.
- Retained Earnings.
- Intercompany Accounts.

Account mappings may vary by company, branch, transaction type, item category, tax category, and other configured dimensions.

## 4. Sales Accounting Events

### 4.1 Sales Invoice

When an approved credit sales invoice is posted:

- Debit: Accounts Receivable.
- Credit: Sales Revenue.
- Credit: Output Tax, where applicable.

### 4.2 Cash Sales

For a cash sale:

- Debit: Cash or Bank.
- Credit: Sales Revenue.
- Credit: Output Tax, where applicable.

### 4.3 Cost of Goods Sold

When inventory is issued for a sale:

- Debit: Cost of Goods Sold.
- Credit: Inventory.

The inventory cost must be calculated using the configured inventory valuation method.

### 4.4 Sales Return

For an approved sales return:

- Debit: Sales Returns.
- Debit: Output Tax Reversal, where applicable.
- Credit: Accounts Receivable or Customer Refund Payable.

For returned goods accepted back into inventory:

- Debit: Inventory.
- Credit: Cost of Goods Sold.

The inventory return cost must follow the applicable costing policy.

### 4.5 Customer Receipt

When customer funds are received:

- Debit: Bank or Cash.
- Credit: Customer Receipts Clearing or Accounts Receivable, depending on the configured receipt and allocation process.

Customer receipt allocation must be recorded separately from the physical bank transaction where necessary.

### 4.6 Sales Discounts

Discount accounting must distinguish between:
- Trade Discounts.
- Invoice Discounts.
- Early Payment Discounts.
- Settlement Discounts.

The accounting and tax treatment must follow the applicable accounting policy and local tax regulations.

## 5. Procurement and Accounts Payable Events

### 5.1 Goods Receipt Before Supplier Invoice

When goods are received before the supplier invoice:

- Debit: Inventory.
- Credit: GRNI.

### 5.2 Supplier Invoice Against GRNI

When the supplier invoice is received and matched with the goods receipt:

- Debit: GRNI.
- Debit: Recoverable Input Tax, where applicable.
- Debit or Credit: Purchase Price Variance or other applicable differences.
- Credit: Accounts Payable.

### 5.3 Direct Supplier Invoice Without GRN

For non-stock purchases or services:

- Debit: Relevant Expense, Asset, or Other Account.
- Debit: Recoverable Input Tax, where applicable.
- Credit: Accounts Payable.

### 5.4 Supplier Advance Payment

When an advance payment is made:

- Debit: Supplier Advances.
- Credit: Bank or Cash.

When the advance is allocated against a supplier invoice:

- Debit: Accounts Payable.
- Credit: Supplier Advances.

### 5.5 Supplier Payment

When a supplier payment is executed:

- Debit: Accounts Payable.
- Credit: Bank or Cash.

### 5.6 Purchase Return

For an approved purchase return:

- Debit: Accounts Payable or Supplier Refund Receivable.
- Credit: Inventory or Relevant Expense.
- Credit: Recoverable Input Tax Reversal, where applicable.

The exact treatment must depend on whether the original purchase was capitalized as inventory, recorded as an expense, or recognized as an asset.

## 6. Inventory Accounting Events

### 6.1 Inventory Receipt

Inventory receipts must follow the procurement and GRNI accounting process.

### 6.2 Inventory Issue for Internal Consumption

When inventory is issued for an expense or internal use:

- Debit: Relevant Expense or Asset Account.
- Credit: Inventory.

### 6.3 Inventory Transfer

Transfers between warehouses belonging to the same company generally do not change the company's total inventory value.

The system must maintain quantity and location movements without creating unnecessary revenue or expense entries.

Transfers between separate legal entities must follow the applicable intercompany accounting process.

### 6.4 Inventory Write-Down

When inventory is written down to Net Realizable Value:

- Debit: Inventory Write-Down Expense.
- Credit: Inventory Allowance or Inventory.

The system must support reversals of write-downs when permitted under IAS 2.

### 6.5 Inventory Revaluation and Cost Adjustments

The system must support approved inventory cost adjustments and their corresponding accounting entries.

Accounting treatment must depend on the adjustment reason and applicable accounting policy.

### 6.6 Inventory Reconciliation

Inventory valuation must be reconciled against the corresponding General Ledger control accounts.

## 7. Import and Landed Cost Accounting

### 7.1 Import Advance Payment

- Debit: Supplier Advances or Import Advances.
- Credit: Bank.

### 7.2 Import Goods Receipt

Where goods are received before the supplier invoice:

- Debit: Inventory.
- Credit: GRNI.

### 7.3 Eligible Landed Costs

Eligible costs directly attributable to bringing inventory to its present location and condition must be capitalized in accordance with IAS 2.

Examples include qualifying freight, insurance, customs duties, and handling costs.

The system must distinguish recoverable taxes and non-capitalizable expenses from eligible inventory costs.

### 7.4 Landed Cost Allocation

Approved landed costs must be allocated to the relevant inventory items using the configured allocation method.

Late landed cost adjustments must be allocated between remaining inventory and Cost of Goods Sold, where applicable.

### 7.5 Import Bank Charges and LC Costs

Bank charges, LC commissions, and financing costs must be classified according to their nature and applicable accounting policy.

Not every import-related bank charge is automatically capitalizable.

## 8. Manufacturing Accounting Events

### 8.1 Raw Material Issue to Production

- Debit: Work in Progress.
- Credit: Raw Materials Inventory.

### 8.2 Direct Labor

- Debit: Work in Progress.
- Credit: Payroll Clearing or Relevant Payroll Liability.

### 8.3 Production Overheads

Eligible production overheads must be allocated to WIP based on configured allocation rules.

### 8.4 Finished Goods Completion

- Debit: Finished Goods Inventory.
- Credit: Work in Progress.

### 8.5 Production Scrap and Variances

Scrap and production variances must be recorded according to the configured costing method and accounting policy.

The system must distinguish normal production losses from abnormal losses.

### 8.6 Subcontracting

Subcontracting costs must be recorded and allocated to WIP or the relevant inventory cost according to the applicable production costing policy.

## 9. Fixed Assets Accounting Events

### 9.1 Asset Acquisition

When an asset is acquired:

- Debit: Fixed Assets or Capital Work in Progress.
- Credit: Accounts Payable, GRNI, or the relevant clearing account.

### 9.2 Depreciation

- Debit: Depreciation Expense.
- Credit: Accumulated Depreciation.

### 9.3 Asset Improvement

Qualifying capital improvements:

- Debit: Fixed Assets.
- Credit: Accounts Payable or Bank.

Routine repairs and maintenance must be expensed.

### 9.4 Asset Impairment

- Debit: Impairment Loss.
- Credit: Asset or Accumulated Impairment Account.

### 9.5 Asset Disposal

The system must:
- Remove the asset's original cost.
- Remove accumulated depreciation.
- Recognize disposal proceeds.
- Calculate the resulting gain or loss.
- Record applicable taxes where required.

## 10. Treasury and Banking Accounting Events

### 10.1 Bank Deposit

- Debit: Bank.
- Credit: Cash or the relevant clearing account.

### 10.2 Bank Transfer

- Debit: Receiving Bank Account.
- Credit: Sending Bank Account.

Bank charges and foreign exchange differences must be recorded separately where applicable.

### 10.3 Bank Charges

- Debit: Bank Charges Expense.
- Credit: Bank.

### 10.4 Customer Cheques

Receiving a cheque must not automatically classify it as available bank cash.

The system must distinguish cheque custody, collection, and actual bank collection.

### 10.5 Loan Receipt

- Debit: Bank.
- Credit: Loan Liability.

### 10.6 Loan Repayment

- Debit: Loan Principal Liability.
- Debit: Interest Expense.
- Credit: Bank.

The principal and interest portions must be calculated separately.

### 10.7 Foreign Exchange Differences

Realized and unrealized exchange differences must be recorded according to the applicable accounting standards and configured accounting policies.

## 11. HR and Payroll Accounting Events

### 11.1 Payroll Recognition

At payroll approval and posting:

- Debit: Salary and Related Expenses.
- Credit: Net Salary Payable.
- Credit: Employee Tax Payable.
- Credit: Employee Social Insurance Payable.
- Credit: Other Employee Deduction Liabilities.

### 11.2 Employer Contributions

- Debit: Employer Contribution Expense.
- Credit: Employer Social Insurance Payable.

### 11.3 Salary Payment

- Debit: Net Salary Payable.
- Credit: Bank.

### 11.4 Employee Advances

When an employee advance is paid:

- Debit: Employee Advances Receivable.
- Credit: Bank or Cash.

When deducted from payroll:

- Debit: Net Salary Payable.
- Credit: Employee Advances Receivable.

The system must prevent duplicate recognition of payroll expenses and liabilities.

## 12. Tax Accounting Events

The Tax Management module must support configurable accounting treatment for:

- Output VAT.
- Recoverable Input VAT.
- Non-Recoverable Input VAT.
- Withholding Tax.
- Payroll Tax.
- Other applicable taxes and statutory liabilities.

Tax accounting must depend on the relevant transaction, tax category, legal entity, jurisdiction, and effective-dated tax rules.

Tax rules must not be hard-coded into the posting engine.

## 13. Intercompany Accounting Events

The system must support reciprocal accounting entries between related companies.

Example: Company A sells goods to Company B.

Company A:
- Debit: Intercompany Receivable.
- Credit: Intercompany Sales Revenue.
- Record applicable inventory cost.

Company B:
- Debit: Inventory or Relevant Expense.
- Credit: Intercompany Payable.

The system must link both sides of the transaction and support reconciliation and elimination during consolidation.

## 14. Consolidation Accounting

The consolidation module must support:

- Intercompany balance eliminations.
- Intercompany revenue and expense eliminations.
- Unrealized profit in closing inventory eliminations.
- Investment in subsidiary eliminations.
- Non-controlling interests.
- Foreign currency translation adjustments.
- Consolidation adjustments and audit trails.

Consolidation entries must be maintained separately from the original company ledgers.

## 15. Posting Controls and Validation

Before generating or posting a journal entry, the system must validate:

- Source document approval.
- Correct tenant and company context.
- Open accounting period.
- Valid account mappings.
- Active posting accounts.
- Required accounting dimensions.
- Correct transaction and functional currency values.
- Balanced debit and credit totals.
- Duplicate event prevention.
- User authorization and approval limits.
- Tax calculation and classification, where applicable.

## 16. Accounting Configuration and Version Control

The system must allow authorized administrators to configure accounting rules by:

- Company.
- Transaction Type.
- Item Category.
- Tax Category.
- Branch.
- Effective Date.
- Accounting Policy.

Changes to accounting configurations must be versioned and audited.

Historical transactions must retain the accounting rules applicable at their original posting date.

## 17. Testing Requirements

The system must include automated tests for:

- Every major accounting event.
- Correct debit and credit account determination.
- Balanced journal entries.
- Partial invoices and partial receipts.
- Partial payments and advances.
- Sales and purchase returns.
- Inventory valuation and write-downs.
- Landed cost capitalization and adjustments.
- Production WIP and variances.
- Fixed asset depreciation and disposal.
- Payroll recognition and settlement.
- Foreign currency transactions.
- Intercompany transactions.
- Consolidation eliminations.
- Closed accounting periods.
- Duplicate accounting event prevention.
- Reversal and correction entries.

## 18. Important Implementation Rule

The examples in this document define expected accounting patterns, not fixed account codes or universal accounting treatments.

The final accounting result must depend on:
- Applicable accounting standards.
- Local tax and legal regulations.
- Company accounting policies.
- Configured account mappings.
- Transaction circumstances.

All accounting events must pass through the Central Accounting Posting Engine and maintain complete source-document traceability.
